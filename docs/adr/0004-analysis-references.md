# ADR 0004 — Two references for strengths and weaknesses

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-29
- **Implementation:** milestone M4a (the match page), then M4b (the damage by source, in the damage and build tab of the match page), then M4c (the review of the last matches).

## Context

TheLibrary must tell the player, for each match, what went well and what to work on. A value alone ("612 souls per minute") means nothing without a reference.

## Decision

Compare every metric with **two references**:

1. **Community** — percentiles (p1–p99) of Paige players in the user's rank bracket over the last 30 days, from `GET /v1/analytics/player-stats/metrics`, restricted to a **5-minute match-duration bucket** (many metrics are per-match totals: the median teammate healing of Paige players doubles between 25–30 and 40–45-minute matches).
2. **Personal** — the median of the 20 previous Paige matches, with the median absolute deviation (MAD) as spread.

Both signals are oriented so that higher means better, combined into a score (60% community, 40% personal), and the top 3 strengths and weaknesses are shown with their evidence and a confidence level. Insights are computed on the fly, never stored.

## As implemented (M4a)

The decision stands. The match page of milestone M4a settled the points it left open:

- **The cohort:** a ranked match is compared with the tier of the badge the player had in it, any other match with the tier of their current rank (refreshed once a day), and a player with neither with every rank. The community is the whole tier, filtered on the match average badge (`tier × 10 + 1` to `tier × 10 + 6`), which only ranked matches have; the other filters are the hero, the normal mode, the last 30 days and the duration window of the match (under 20 minutes, five minutes wide from 20 to 45 minutes, then 45 minutes and longer). A cohort needs at least 200 Paige matches (`MIN_COHORT_MATCHES`): a tier with fewer gives way to every rank in the same duration window, and when that is too small as well the match has no community comparison, which the page says. The data of a cohort is kept 24 hours, entries older than 7 days are purged, and an expired copy is still used when the app is offline.
- **The phases:** 0:00–10:00, 10:00–25:00 and 25:00 to the end, each with the souls, the damage to heroes, the damage to objectives and the kills of the player and their share of the team (shown, not scored); a phase under 3 minutes is shown but not scored. The performance curve gives the average and the spread of a counter at each time of the match, not percentiles, so a phase is compared with the community's rate over the same span on both sides: from the start of the phase to its end or to the last curve point that every match of the duration window reaches, whichever comes first. The spread of a gain is `√(σ_end² − σ_start²)` (a gain is taken as independent of where the player started) and the percentile is `100 × Φ(z)`, with `z` the distance of the player's rate to the community's in spreads. That percentile is a known approximation for skewed counters (kills, damage to objectives): `z` is the more faithful figure, and the page words the distance from it (well above, above, around, below, well below).
- **The habits:** the 20 most recent earlier matches that have a value, and at least 5 of them, else there is no personal comparison. The totals that grow with the length of a match (deaths, last hits, denies, healing, barriers) are compared per minute and shown back as totals, so that a long match does not stand out from the habits only by being long.
- **The score:** the community signal is `(p − 50) / 50` and the personal one `z / 2` limited to −1 and 1, both turned so that higher is better. The score is 60% of the first and 40% of the second; a lone signal is the score as it is, unweighted, so that the first matches of a history, and the lane difference at 10:00 (which has no community reference), are not undersold. A strength is in the best 30% of its cohort or one robust deviation above the habits, with a score above 0; a weakness is the mirror. The summary keeps at most three of each, and only one per group: metrics that say the same thing (souls per minute, souls at 10:00 and the souls of a phase) share a group, and only the strongest of them shows.
- **The confidence:** three levels. Low for a match under 15 minutes or with a leaver, medium when one of the two references is missing, high otherwise.
- **When a match is analysed:** every archived match is analysed at the end of each synchronisation. After the detail queue, the run projects every archived match that has no projection of the current `PROJECTION_VERSION`, the most recent first, so the matches are analysed before their page is opened. A run that ends early (the network is down, the account is protected) projects too: the projection works from the archives on disk, so it needs no network and no request budget. A page that opens a match still not analysed (before the end of the run that archived it, or after a new version and before the next run) analyses it as a fallback, while a dialog says that the analysis is under way: it projects the match and the 20 archived matches before it that have no projection (the habits are made of them), then shows the analysis. The 20 leave out the matches the analysis could not read, so opening the page again finds nothing left to project, and a match first projected as one of the 20 of another page has its own 20 completed the first time its page is opened, the same way. The habits keep their rule over what is projected: a metric that is often unknown (the late phase of a short match, the lane) may have fewer values than the habits need, and then has no personal comparison. The projections run one at a time, whoever asks (the synchronisation or a page), so two never write the same match at once. The analysis of a page runs in the exclusive section of the synchronisation: an account change waits for it, and a new analysis is refused while the account changes, so an analysis never writes for an account that is being erased or switched.
- **What is stored:** the insights are computed when the page opens. Only the values of the metrics are stored, once per archived match (see [ADR 0003](0003-user-centric-archive-zstd.md)): a match already projected with the current `PROJECTION_VERSION` is not projected again. When that version is bumped, each match is projected again from its archive, with no network access, the same way: at the end of the next synchronisation (a page opened before it analyses its own match, as the fallback above).
- **Critical shots:** a critical-shot rate was considered and dropped. Paige almost never lands a critical shot (the community's third quartile is 0), so the metric could only add noise, and the API's definition of the rate differs from the app's own formula, so the two could not be compared like for like.

## As implemented (M4b)

The decision stands for the metrics. The damage and build tab of the match page adds the player's damage to heroes by source (weapon, melee, abilities, items), and it has **one reference for now: the player's own habits**. The community comparison of the damage sources is to come.

- **The shares:** a source is read as its share of the damage the player dealt to enemy heroes in the match, from the damage matrix of the archive (the player's own damage to the heroes of the other team, without the category rows that add the sources up a second time). The shares of all the sources add up to 100 %. The critical hits of a source (`<name>_crit`) count with the source: a weapon is one line, not two.
- **The share while owned:** a shop item is not compared on the whole match, since an item bought late would always look weak. Its share is its own damage over the damage the player dealt, all sources together, **while they owned it**: from its purchase to its sale, or to the end of the match when it was kept (the periods of an item bought several times are merged where they overlap). That damage is read on the total of the damage matrix, interpolated linearly between its samples and from an implicit start of 0 damage at 0:00 before the first one; the statistic snapshots are too coarse for it. The share is capped at 100 %, since the damage while owned is interpolated and can fall short of the item's own.
- **The lines:** the weapon and the melee attack always have a line (at 0 % when unused), and so does every other source that dealt damage in the match. A signature ability has one when it dealt damage in this match, or in an earlier match of the lookup below where it was unlocked, even if it did not in this one; an ability that never deals damage (Plot Armor, say) never shows. An ability that was not unlocked in the match shows "Not unlocked" and is not compared. A source is told apart by the hero's loadout first (the weapon, the melee attack and the four signature abilities, from the hero catalogue), then by the item catalogue (a shop item); anything else (another hero's ability, the Zip Line) is a plain damage source.
- **The habit:** the median of the share a source is compared on (its share of the match's damage, or its share while owned for an item) over the 20 most recent earlier matches **where the source appears** (`PERSONAL_WINDOW`), within the same lookup of the 100 most recent earlier matches as the habits of the metrics (`PREVIOUS_LOOKUP`, five times the window: a source is often missing from a match), and at least 5 of them (`MIN_PERSONAL_MATCHES`), else there is no habit and no comparison. A source appears in a match when it can be used there: the weapon and the melee always, an ability when it was unlocked, a shop item when the player owned it and dealt damage while owning it (whether or not the item itself dealt any; an item bought at the very end has no share), any other source when it dealt damage. A match where it does not appear says nothing about it and does not count.
- **When a source stands out:** its share is at least 5 points away from the habit (`DAMAGE_SHARE_MIN_GAP`) **and**, when the habit varies, at least one robust deviation away too, the same z-score and the same threshold (`STRONG_Z`) as the habits of the metrics. When the habit does not vary at all (a median absolute deviation of 0) there is no deviation to read, and the 5 points alone decide. The gap is needed on top of the deviation because a source that always deals 3 % has a tiny spread, so 3.5 % would be many deviations away although it is only half a point. The line is then marked "more than usual" or "less than usual".
- **What is stored:** the values are computed at the end of each synchronisation, by the projection, and stored per match next to the metrics (since `PROJECTION_VERSION` 2; version 3 added the values of the review, see M4c): `damage_source.<class name>` (the damage of a source over the match; none for a source that dealt none), `item_owned_damage.<item id>` (the damage the player dealt while they owned that shop item) and `ability_unlocked_at.<item id>` (the second the ability was unlocked). Bumping the version to 2 made every archived match be projected again from its archive, with no network access, at the next synchronisation. A match without a damage matrix, or with one that cannot be read, gets no damage values and is projected all the same: its metrics do not depend on them. The shares, the kinds and the habits are not stored but computed when the tab opens, with the catalogues, so the damage lines come from the stored rows and not from the archive (only the final build is read from it).

## As implemented (M4c)

The decision stands, at the scale of a series of matches. The review sums up the player's **last 20 analysed matches** (`REVIEW_MATCHES`) and sets every point of them against two references: the Paige players of the **tier above** the player's own (the community), and the **20 matches before** the last ones (the progress, in place of the habits before a single match). The points are the 24 metrics of the match page, the deaths of each phase, the twelve resistance items, the places among the twelve players and Valve's top three; the damage by source is shown with the matches before as its only reference.

- **The tier above:** the review compares the player with the Paige players of the rank tier above the one of their current rank, any sub-rank (a player in Sentinel 2 is compared with the whole Mystic tier). Only the current rank counts: the review looks at the player as they are now, whatever rank each of the last matches was played at. Eternus has no tier above and stays on its own tier, and a player with no rank is compared with every rank; the page says which of the three it is (`tier_above`, `own_tier`, `every_rank`). The community is filtered as in M4a (the match average badge of the tier, the hero, the normal mode, the last 30 days), and a tier with fewer than 200 Paige matches (`MIN_COHORT_MATCHES`) gives way to every rank.
- **Each match against its own length:** a match is placed as on the match page, with the tier's data for its own duration window, so a 24-minute match and a 41-minute one are not compared with the same community. The cohorts the last 20 matches need are their distinct windows (seven at most). They are downloaded when the page opens and finds that some match has no tier data, that the data is a day old or that the item statistics are missing or a day old, one after the other and never side by side, the window that holds the most matches first (the more recent match settles a tie), then the item statistics: when the budget or a pause of deadlock-api stops the downloads, what most of the matches rest on has been downloaded first. A cohort costs three analytics requests (four when its tier falls back to every rank) and the item statistics two (three), so the first opening in a day can take about 30, which the analytics budget of 30 a minute (`ANALYTICS_RATE_LIMIT`) is sized for. Everything is kept a day and read back whatever its age, so reading the review downloads no community data and works offline once the game catalogues are cached. The curve of a cohort now holds the deaths, so the cohorts are stored under a new key (`analytics.cohort.v2.<cohort>`): the entries stored before are never read again and are purged once a week old.
- **The deaths of each phase:** they are read on the `deaths` of the community curve, over the same stretch the curve covers on both sides, the rule of the phases of M4a: the phase as played, up to the time every match of the duration window reaches. The early and mid games count deaths per match, and only in the matches that played the whole phase (to 10:00 and to 25:00): a match that ended in the middle of a phase would look better only for being shorter. The late game has no end of its own, so it counts deaths per 10 minutes. The player's deaths over the covered stretch are stored with the match (`phase_deaths.<phase>.community_window`) and the match is placed on the curve like the phase metrics of M4a, fewer deaths being better. A phase of a match without complete death details has no value, never 0.
- **The resistance items:** the twelve are a fixed list by class name, since the resistance properties of the item catalogue cannot tell the player's own protection from the auras and side stats of other items; the catalogue only names them. Each has two points. _When it is bought_: the minute the player first buys it in a match is placed among the purchases of the tier by minute (the share of purchases made earlier plus half of those made in the same minute, earlier being better) and compared with the mean minute the tier buys it at. The purchases come from the item statistics by minute of the game (`/v1/analytics/item-stats`, with `min_matches=1` so that the early and late tails of a rarely bought item are kept), asked once for the tier over matches of any length, since the review compares a series of matches and not one length. _How often_: one in a match that bought the item, zero in one that did not (told only in a match whose items are known), against the share of the tier's matches that bought it (its purchases over its matches, at most all of them); buying more or less of an item is neither better nor worse, so this point has no mark. Two more points are about the first resistance item, whichever it is: its minute, and the deaths before it. A match that bought shop items but no resistance item counts all its deaths before one it never bought.
- **The marks and the summary:** each match is judged on its own, and its oriented placement makes it weak (the 30th percentile or lower) or strong (the 70th or higher), as in M4a. A few points have a fixed rule instead of a placement: the lane gap at 10:00 (500 souls behind or ahead), a place among the twelve players (10th to 12th is weak, 1st to 3rd strong), Valve's top three (strong in it, nothing outside it) and the deaths before the first resistance item (weak from 3, strong at 0). A point is listed as a weakness when it is weak in more matches than it is strong and in at least a quarter of the matches that have a mark for it (`REVIEW_MIN_FLAGGED_SHARE`), and has a mark in at least 5 matches (`MIN_PERSONAL_MATCHES`), so that an item bought once is not listed from that one match; a strength is the mirror. There is no cut: every flagged point is in the summary and in its own tab, the most often flagged first.
- **The figures:** the player's figure is the median of the values of the matches for the metrics of the match page, since the reference of those is made of percentiles, and their mean for the other points (the deaths of a phase, the buy minute and the pick rate of an item, the places among the players), whose tier figure, where there is one, is a mean. The gap is like for like: the player's figure and the tier's are both made of the matches that were compared with the tier, each on its own duration window and stretch, so a match with no tier data counts in the player's figure but not in the gap, and the row says when the two figures differ. A gap smaller than one standard error of the player's compared figure (`PROGRESS_MIN_Z`, the tier's figure taken as exact) is within the noise of the matches: the row writes "about the same" rather than its decimal. No figure is given from fewer than 5 values (`MIN_PERSONAL_MATCHES`), whichever it is; the counts are always given.
- **The progress:** the last 20 matches against the 20 before, around the same centre. A change counts when it is at least one standard error of the difference between the two centres (`PROGRESS_MIN_Z`), otherwise the figure is stable; each window needs 5 values, and a point that is neither better nor worse when higher (a damage share, a pick rate) has no direction.
- **When the tier cannot tell:** what it cannot tell is said and never replaced by a number. A match whose tier data is not cached, or not for its length, counts in the player's figure with no mark, and the page says how many. It also says how many matches were compared with every rank because their tier holds too few of their length, and when the data is a day old or more.
- **What is stored:** the review values are computed by the projection, at the end of each synchronisation, and stored per match next to the metrics (`PROJECTION_VERSION` 3): `phase_deaths.<phase>` (all the deaths of the player in the phase, whoever caused them) and its `.community_window`, `item_bought_at.<item id>` (the second a shop item was first bought), `deaths_before_item.<item id>`, `scoreboard_place.<figure>` with `scoreboard_place_of.<figure>` (the place among the players and how many have the figure) and `valve_rank` (1 to 3, or 0 outside the top three). A value that is not known is not written: the deaths when the death details of the archive are incomplete, the items and the places when the archive holds none. A match with no shop purchase stored at all has unknown items, not an empty shopping list, so it says nothing about the pick rate of an item. Bumping the version to 3 made every archived match be projected again from its archive, with no network access, at the next synchronisation. The thresholds of the review act when the page is shown, on the stored values, so tuning one needs no new projection.
- **Why community matches are not downloaded yet:** the minute of the first resistance item, the deaths before it and the damage by source have no figure for the tier above: they are the player's own figures, set against the 20 matches before. An exact figure would need a sample of community matches, downloaded and read as the player's own are. The item statistics count purchases by item and minute, so they cannot tell which of the twelve items a player bought first, and a sample of matches is a download of many matches where the review needs a few requests. It is left for later.

## Consequences

- Objective standing _and_ personal progression, with explainable evidence.
- The community data is cached for 24 hours per wanted cohort (rank tier and duration window): three analytics requests when a cohort is not cached yet or is a day old (four when a small tier falls back to every rank), none otherwise.
- The review asks for the cohort of the tier above for each duration window of the last 20 matches, and for the item statistics of that tier: up to about 30 analytics requests the first time it opens in a day, spread one after the other within the budget of 30 a minute, none afterwards until the data is a day old.
- Thresholds and weights live in one file, `src/core/analysis/insights/thresholds.ts`, and can be tuned without touching the rules.

## Alternatives considered

- **Community only** — objective, but hides progress.
- **Personal only** — no analytics calls, but no idea of where the player stands.
